# Prevention of Sexual Harassment (PoSH)

**Sources:** MountBlue PoSH document, Sexual Harassment Overview video, Bullying and Harassment video, Sexual Harassment scenarios video.

---

## 1. What kinds of behaviour cause sexual harassment?

Sexual harassment is any **unwelcome** verbal, visual or physical conduct of a sexual nature that is severe or pervasive, or that creates a hostile work environment. **Intent doesn't matter; the impact on the person does.**

### Forms of behaviour

- **Verbal**
  - Comments on someone's body or clothes
  - Sexual or gender-based jokes and innuendos
  - Repeatedly asking someone out after they've said no
  - Spreading rumours about someone's personal or sexual life
  - Asking about someone's sexual experiences
  - Obscene language
- **Visual / digital**
  - Sexual posters, cartoons or screen savers
  - Sexual emails, texts or voicemails
  - Cyberbullying through email, messages or social media
- **Physical**
  - Unwelcome touching, hugging, patting, stroking or brushing against someone
  - Blocking someone's movement
  - Molestation or assault
  - Leering, staring or sexual gestures

### Two categories

- **Quid pro quo ("this for that"):** a person in authority offers promotions or raises, or threatens demotion or firing, in exchange for sexual favours.
- **Hostile work environment:** repeated unwelcome behaviour that makes the workplace intimidating or offensive and affects someone's work. This is the more common type.

### Other behaviours to avoid

- **Gender-based discrimination**
  - Stereotyping
  - Giving women extra work or men better assignments
  - Blocking promotions
  - Not taking complaints seriously
- **General bullying and harassment**
  - Yelling and name-calling
  - Humiliating or intimidating someone
  - Harmful hazing

### Key points

- It can come from a supervisor, co-worker, vendor, client or customer.
- It can be same-gender or opposite-gender.
- It can count even if the target didn't object, since a bystander who is offended can be affected.
- Normal management decisions about duties, deadlines and performance are **not** harassment.

## 2. What would I do if I face or witness such behaviour?

### If I'm facing it

1. Tell the person clearly and respectfully that the behaviour is unwelcome and ask them to stop.
2. Keep a record of dates, times, places, witnesses, what happened and how I responded.
3. If it continues, or I'm not comfortable confronting them, report it **in writing to an ICC member**:
   - Bipasha Agarwal (Presiding Officer): bipasha@mountblue.io
   - N Gangadhar Reddy: gangadhar@mountblue.io
   - Konark: konark@mountblue.io
4. If the circumstances warrant it, file a police complaint.

### If I witness it

- Reporting is a duty, not optional.
- A co-worker or anyone who knows about the incident can file a complaint with the ICC.
- Support the affected person, note what I saw, and report it.

### What to expect after reporting

- MountBlue has a **zero-tolerance** policy.
- The offender can face:
  - Warning or written apology
  - Withheld promotion or increments
  - Counselling
  - Salary deduction
  - Termination
- Retaliation against someone who complains is not allowed.
- The complaint is kept as confidential as possible.
- Frivolous or false complaints are also penalised.

### Prevention in my own behaviour

- If I wouldn't say or do it with a family member standing next to me, I don't do it at work.
- Avoid touching unless I'm sure it's welcome.
- Stop immediately if someone shows discomfort (not laughing, pulling back, walking away).
- "I meant it as a joke" is not a defence.
