### 1. What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?
- Avoid getting distracted by one's own thoughts and focus of the topic and speaker instead.
- Try not to interrupt the other person, let them finish and then respond.
- Use door openers, the phrases which show your interest and make the other person know you're attentively listening to them. 
- Show that you're listening with body language.
- If appropriate, take notes during the conversations. Paraphrase what others have said to make sure you're on the same page.
- Make sure you make eye contact with the speaker to let them know you are their active audience.
  
### 2. According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?
- Listen to understand: focus on the speaker’s actual message and feelings, not your own opinion.
- Repeat it back in your own words: restate what you heard (paraphrase or mirror).
- Check if you got it right: ask the speaker to confirm or correct you.
- Don’t add your own judgment or advice: only reflect what was said.
- Let the speaker clarify: if you were wrong, they explain again and you reflect again.
- Repeat until both sides agree that the message was understood correctly.
- Pay attention to emotions and body language, not just words.

### 3. What are the obstacles in your listening process?

- Getting distracted by my phone or notifications.
- Thinking about my reply while the other person is still talking.
- Jumping to conclusions before they finish.
- Losing focus in long or boring sessions.
- Being tired or stressed, so my mind wanders.
- Noisy surroundings.
- Judging the speaker or the topic early (“I already know this”).
  
### 4. What can you do to improve your listening?

- Keep my phone on silent and out of reach during conversations and meetings.
- Wait 2-3 seconds before replying so I don’t interrupt.
- Take notes during meetings and technical discussions.
- Paraphrase key points back to confirm understanding.
- Ask questions instead of assuming.
- Maintain eye contact and stay present.
- Get enough sleep so I can focus better.
  
### 5. When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day to day life?

- When talking to seniors, teachers, or interviewers and I’m afraid of saying the wrong thing.
- When I want to avoid conflict or arguments.
- When I’m in a group where everyone disagrees with me, so I stay quiet.
- When I’m unsure of my knowledge and don’t want to look wrong.
- When I say “it’s fine” even though it’s not.
  
### 6. When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day to day life?

- When I’m very stressed, tired, or frustrated.
- When someone repeatedly ignores my point or interrupts me.
- When I feel disrespected or treated unfairly.
- When I’ve held things in for too long and they burst out.
- Signs: raised voice, cutting people off, blunt or harsh words.
  
### 7. When do you switch into Passive Aggressive (sarcasm/gossiping/taunts/silent treatment and others) communication styles in your day to day life?

- Sarcasm: when I’m annoyed but don’t want to say it directly.
- Silent treatment: when I’m upset and avoid talking instead of explaining.
- Gossiping: venting to others instead of talking to the person involved.
- Taunts or indirect hints: “Nice of you to finally show up.”
- Slow or half-hearted compliance: doing a task badly because I disagree with it.
- It usually happens when I want to avoid direct confrontation but still show I’m unhappy.
  
### 8. How can you make your communication assertive? You can watch and analyse the videos, then think what would be a few steps you can apply in your own life? 

- Use “I” statements: “I feel stressed when the deadline changes suddenly” instead of “You always change things.”
- Be clear and direct: say exactly what I need or think, without over-apologizing or hinting.
- Stay calm: take a breath before responding when I’m upset.
- Keep a steady tone and confident body language: eye contact, relaxed posture, normal volume.
- Say “no” politely but firmly: “I can’t take this on right now, but I can help next week.”
- Respect the other person’s view too: listen, then state my own point.
- Speak up early: address small issues before they build into aggression.
- Separate the problem from the person: focus on the behavior or issue, not attacking them.
- Be willing to compromise: aim for a win-win outcome.
